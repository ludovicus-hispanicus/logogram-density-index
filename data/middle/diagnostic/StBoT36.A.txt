---
discipline: diagnostic omens
period: Middle Babylonian
provenance: Ḫattuša (Boğazköy)
recension: Anatolian periphery
counting: line
topic: prognosis (death/recovery), fever, hands/feet, face, nose-breath, eyes; Hand-of-DN diagnoses (Šulak, rābiṣu, Ištar, ghost)
series: pre-canonical Late Bronze Age diagnostic omens; the largest Ḫattuša tablet (Tafel A); colophon ul qati 'not finished' shows the omens were already in a tablet series (Wilhelm 1994:4)
canonical_parallel: many individual entries parallel canonical Sakikkû/TDP (Wilhelm 1994 commentary cross-refs TDP throughout); cf. Schmidtchen 2021 concordance
edition: Wilhelm 1994, StBoT 36, 21-33; Tafel A = KUB 37,193 (+) KBo 36,51 (+) KBo 36,50 + KUB 34,6 (Büyükkale)
dating: second half of the Hittite Empire period (13th century BCE), by paleography (Wilhelm 1994:5-8). Counted here as Middle Babylonian (chronological horizon), western/Anatolian periphery.
note: >
  Transliteration after Wilhelm's sign-level Umschrift (2010... 1994:21-23),
  directly scoreable. AKKADIAN ONLY: the Hittite and Luwian interlinear
  GLOSSES (marked \\ in the edition) are OMITTED, since they are not Akkadian.
  Written by Hittite-school scribes; the syllabary shows the peripheral
  ('Randgebiete') values (Wilhelm 1994:9), and symptom-protases are spelled
  syllabically where Babylonian uses logograms (e-em not KÚM, kaṣ-ṣí not SED).
  Diagnoses stay logographic (BA.ÚŠ, ŠU {d}DN). Logograms uppercase, syllabic
  lowercase, {} determinative, [] restoration, x illegible. counting:line is
  used only for segmentation; the LDI is a token ratio, independent of it.
  Heavily fragmentary; only preserved Akkadian lines are given.
---
